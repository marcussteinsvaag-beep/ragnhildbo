Background

About

I am a researcher at the Department of Psychology, University of Oslo, in the Section for Cognitive and Clinical Neuroscience. My work focuses on how the brain’s cognitive systems - attention, executive functions and emotion regulation - affect mental health and treatment outcomes.

A central part of my research concerns attention bias modification (ABM) - training programmes that aim to change attentional biases in people with depression. I also work on digital self-help tools for reducing alcohol use, and on the cognitive consequences of COVID-19.

I have a clinical background as a psychologist at the Norwegian Labour and Welfare Administration’s (NAV) competence centre for vocational rehabilitation, with experience of complex neuropsychological assessments.

In 2026 I completed the Training Programme for Young Research Leaders, a national leadership programme run by the psychology departments at the Universities of Bergen and Oslo, NTNU and UiT The Arctic University of Norway. Over five workshops in one year, it covered career development as a research leader, communication and research strategy, leading research groups and networks, and external funding.

I am principal investigator of DigiTIFF, funded by the Kavli Trust, and lead the work package on biomarkers and pathological mechanisms in the DRIVE depression research network.

Current work

Research

Principal investigator · Kavli Trust

DigiTIFF

Digital interventions for relapse prevention. Investigates whether digital interventions can prevent or delay relapse in adolescents aged 16–19 who have had depression and/or anxiety. Funded by the Kavli Trust and the University of Oslo, 2025–2029.

About the project →

DEPTREAT

Does training positive attention help in depression? A randomised controlled trial of attention bias modification as a treatment tool for depression.

Affective bias and rumination

The effect of affective bias modification on transdiagnostic rumination and symptoms. A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

TRAINCOVID

Cognitive rehabilitation after COVID-19. A randomised controlled trial testing cognitive training as a treatment for persistent cognitive problems after SARS-CoV-2 infection.

The DRIVE network

Interdisciplinary depression research network at the University of Oslo. I lead the work package on biomarkers and pathological mechanisms.

Digital self-help tools

Exploring the potential and limitations of digital tools for reducing alcohol use in primary care, with an emphasis on accessibility and usability.
